abstract: Using data from Peekbank, an open database of eye-tracking experiments measuring
  children's word recognition, we analyzed 26 datasets from over 2,500 children ages
  6 months to 6 years. Word recognition becomes faster, more accurate, and less variable
  across development, consistent with a process of skill learning. Factor analysis
  revealed covariation of word recognition speed and accuracy with children's vocabulary
  size, and across longitudinal models, speed, accuracy, and vocabulary were coupled.
  Children with overall faster word recognition tended to show faster vocabulary growth,
  though developmental growth in word recognition skill was not specifically associated
  with growth in vocabulary. These findings support viewing word recognition as a
  gradually developing skill intertwined with early language learning.
