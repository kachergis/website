abstract: Newell (1973) predicted that experimental psychology's focus on falsifying
  theories of individual phenomena would not lead to a unifying theory subsuming several
  phenomena. Instead of seeking to detect effects, Newell argued psychologists should
  build predictive theories of behavior by modeling 1) the structure of the task environment
  (i.e., context), 2) the subjects' own goals, and 3) the invariant structure of the
  subjects' processing mechanisms. We explore how video games may serve as the complex
  behavioral paradigm that Newell sought, which is necessary to study in order to
  make progress towards a unified theory of cognition.
